# Jatrabari, Dhaka — research notes for the open world

These notes record the real-world references used while rebuilding the city, the buildings and the
interiors. They are deliberately practical: what a Jatrabari street looks like, how a Dhaka mid-rise
is planned, how its stair core and roof work, and which details make the place read as *Jatrabari*
rather than a generic South Asian city.

## 1. The real geography

| Place in the game | Real reference | What the game keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Jatrabari crossing | Jatrabari Chowrasta, Jatrabari Thana, Dhaka South City Corporation | The wide, congested intersection where the Dhaka–Chattogram Highway, the flyover and the old inner roads meet. |
| Mayor Hanif Flyover | Mayor Mohammad Hanif Flyover (Gulistan–Jatrabari–Kajla) | Elevated concrete deck, toll plaza, ramps, the dark undercroft used by tea stalls and CNG drivers. |
| Kajla | Kajla bus stop / Kajla road, below the flyover | Tea stalls, CNG stands, tyre shops, small workshops, dense 4–7 storey buildings. |
| Dania | Dania, Jatrabari; Dania College | College campus, exam halls, coaching centres, photocopy and stationery shops. |
| Jurain | Jurain, Kadamtali; Jurain railway station and graveyard | Tin-and-brick lanes, the rail line, the graveyard, the pond, low-rent messes. |
| Sontek | Sontek / Shanir Akhra edge of Jatrabari | Mixed commercial-residential buildings, clinics, lawyers' chambers. |
| Adarsha school | Adarsha High School, Jatrabari | A three-storey school with a field, a boundary wall and a guard room. |

Real reference points: Jatrabari Thana is about 13.19 km² and contains 17 mouzas/mahallas; the
Dhaka–Chattogram Highway runs through it. The Mayor Mohammad Hanif Flyover connects Gulistan with
Jatrabari and Kajla. The Dania College and Adarsha High School are real Jatrabari institutions.
The Jatrabari–Kajla road below the flyover is a known bottleneck and is regularly in the news for
its surface condition. (See sources at the end.)

## 2. Street character

* **Widths.** The highway is 6–8 lanes; the flyover deck is 4 lanes with a toll plaza. The old
  inner lanes are 3–6 m wide, often without footpaths. Service lanes under the flyover are 4–6 m.
* **Edges.** Shopfronts open directly onto the road. There is a continuous plinth of 0.15–0.45 m,
  a shutter, a signboard, and often an awning. Upper floors overhang the footpath by 0.3–0.9 m.
* **Services.** Overhead 11 kV lines on concrete poles, tangled 230 V drops, gas risers on facades,
  water tanks on roofs, cable TV and fibre bundles, open drains at the edge.
* **Movement.** Rickshaws, CNGs, buses, motorcycles, handcarts, pedestrians and hawkers share the
  same asphalt. The flyover removes long-distance traffic but concentrates local traffic below.
* **Night.** Shutters down, tube lights and sodium lamps, generators, load-shedding, tea stalls
  under the flyover, CNG headlights, the graveyard and the rail line dark and quiet.

## 3. The Dhaka mid-rise (the building type used in the game)

Most Jatrabari buildings are 4–10 storey reinforced-concrete (RCC) frames with brick infill,
cement plaster, and either a flat roof or a small pitched tin canopy. A typical plot is 5–10 katha
(about 330–660 m²), but the game also uses narrow 3.6–6 m plots and wide 20–35 m landmark plots.

### 3.1 Structure

* RCC columns on a 4.5–6.0 m grid; 0.25–0.4 m square or rectangular columns.
* 125–150 mm slabs; beams 250 × 450 mm; brick infill walls 125/250 mm.
* Ground floor is often open parking (columns only) or shops; the first floor is sometimes a
  duplex/office; upper floors are flats.
* Cantilevered balconies 1.0–1.5 m deep, with 1.0–1.2 m MS grille or RCC parapet.
* Rooftop: stair headroom, lift machine room (in taller buildings), overhead water tank on a
  concrete stand, solar panels, clotheslines, satellite dish, potted plants, sometimes a small
  garden or a tin shed.

### 3.2 The stair core

The stair is the vertical spine of the building and the part the game now models in full:

* A typical residential core is 3.0–4.2 m × 3.0–3.6 m, with a 1.0–1.2 m clear flight width.
* Two flights per storey, a mid-landing, and a 0.9–1.2 m landing at each floor.
* Rise 150–180 mm, going 250–300 mm; 16–20 risers for a 3.0–3.2 m storey.
* A 1.0–1.2 m wide door from the floor landing into the corridor/flat.
* A roof door or hatch at the top, opening into a small headroom; the headroom is usually
  2.2–3.0 m × 2.2–3.0 m and 2.2–2.6 m high.
* The stair is enclosed by 125 mm walls, often with a small high window or a grille for light and
  ventilation. In many buildings the stair sits at the front or rear corner of the plot, not in the
  centre, so the flats can take the better light.

### 3.3 The floor plan

A typical 4–6 storey building on a 7–10 m frontage has two flats per floor, one front and one rear,
or two side-by-side flats around a central stair. A larger 12–18 m frontage has three or four flats
per floor. A flat is usually 55–95 m²:

* living/dining 3.6–4.8 m wide, facing the front balcony;
* 2 bedrooms (3.0–3.6 m × 3.6–4.2 m), one with an attached bath;
* kitchen 2.4–3.0 m × 3.0–3.6 m with a counter, sink, gas point and a small service balcony;
* 1–2 baths (1.5–2.0 m × 2.2–2.6 m), tiled, with a low wall and a floor drain;
* a 1.0–1.4 m wide internal corridor connecting the stair landing, living, bedrooms and kitchen;
* a front balcony 1.0–1.5 m deep with a grille and a clothesline;
* a small prayer corner, a wall clock, a fridge, a TV, ceiling fans, tube lights, and a water jar.

Ground-floor shops are 3.0–4.5 m wide, 4.0–6.0 m deep, with a rolling shutter, a 0.45 m plinth,
a counter, wall shelves, a ceiling fan, and a back room/storeroom. A shophouse has the shop below
and the family's flat above, often with a separate side entrance and a narrow stair.

### 3.4 Roof

* Parapet 0.9–1.2 m, often with square pierced openings or MS grille panels.
* Overhead tank 1.0–2.0 m³ on a 0.6–0.9 m concrete stand; black plastic tanks are common.
* Stair headroom with a steel or wooden door, sometimes a second smaller room.
* Clotheslines between two poles; potted plants; a dish antenna; solar panels on newer buildings.
* The roof is a social space: children play, rice and chillies dry, and people sleep there on hot
  nights. The game therefore makes every roof reachable by the real stair, not by a teleport.

## 4. What this means for the game

1. **Every building gets a front door, a real stair core, a floor slab with a stair opening, flats
   with rooms, and a roof door.** The stair is walkable geometry: 18 risers, two flights, a mid
   landing, a ramp collider under the visual steps so the CharacterBody3D climbs smoothly.
2. **Landmark buildings keep their authored ground floors** (the mess, the tower parking, the
   college office, the clinic, the library, the cyber cafe, the superstore, the lawyer's chamber,
   the police station). The new system adds the upper floors, the stair and the roof.
3. **Ordinary buildings are generated on demand.** The exterior is still a shader-painted box in a
   MultiMesh chunk; when the player opens the front door the game disables that building's box
   collider and builds a full interior in place. The interior is freed when the player leaves, so
   the open world stays cheap.
4. **Flats are laid out around the core.** A corridor runs from the stair landing; front and rear
   flats get a living room, bedroom(s), kitchen, bath, balcony/window and a door onto the corridor.
5. **Windows and doors are animated objects.** Doors have a hinge, a handle, a frame and a collider;
   windows have a sash, glass, a sill and a curtain. They open and close on interaction and some
   open automatically when the player is near.
6. **The roof is part of the building.** The stair headroom, parapet, tank, solar panel, garden and
   clothesline all have colliders; the player can walk to the edge and look down at the street.

region, not by any actor or public figure.*
